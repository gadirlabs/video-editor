# Formats

Three presets for mid-form video. Pick one in `project.json` before editing anything.

The mistake to avoid is editing every video in the same register. The furniture that makes
a tutorial clear — the step labels, the part card, the three-item list — is exactly what
makes a vlog feel corporate. Choosing the preset is choosing what the viewer is doing while
they watch, and everything else follows from it.

## Course

**For:** tutorials and lessons where the viewer follows along and builds something.

The viewer's question is "what do I do now?" Structure and legibility beat atmosphere, and
repetition is a feature: the same part card every episode tells the viewer where they are.

- Structure furniture is welcome and should repeat: a part card at the open, step labels
  during the work, a recap, a next-step card at the close.
- Screen recordings carry the video. Zoom to what matters, hold long enough to read, put a
  callout on the line being discussed. Legibility on a phone is the first test.
- Diagrams earn their place: architecture, data flow, what talks to what.
- One persistent element per episode works well, for instance a board that fills in as the
  build progresses.
- Chapters and corrected captions matter, because people skip back.

**Report:** time with a graphic on screen, target 45 to 65 per cent; longest stretch with no
visual change, target 12 seconds; visible cuts per minute, target under 8.

## Vlog and documentary

**For:** documenting work as it happens. Personable, with teaching inside it.

The viewer's question is "what happened next?" Presence and pace beat structure. The person
and the real footage carry it.

- Furniture is minimal: a lower third once at the start, an occasional date or context
  label, nothing persistent.
- Real footage carries it: the actual screen, the actual product, people, places, things
  going wrong. Your own b-roll beats anything generated or bought.
- Text sits on the frame with a soft shadow, not inside a panel. A word, a date, a number.
- **Avoid here:** step indicators, progress bars, three-item card lists, chapter bumpers
  between sections. That is course vocabulary and it reads as a corporate deck.
- Teaching moments still happen. Give one a single diagram, hold it, return to the person.
  One or two a video, not a system of them.
- Cuts serve pace and story. Cut a beat earlier than feels comfortable.
- Music is usually what carries pace between talking. Set `music` deliberately here.

**Report:** share of running time on real footage other than the talking head, target 25 per
cent or more; longest static talking head, target 10 seconds; graphic events per minute,
target under 4.

## Explainer

**For:** a single idea argued or explained to camera, without a build to follow.

Between the other two. The visual load sits on diagrams and evidence rather than on steps or
on story.

- Diagrams, charts, quotes, screenshots of sources. Each one earns its place by making the
  argument clearer.
- Text on the frame for the load-bearing sentence, with one word set in italic accent.
- No step furniture, no progress element.
- Return to the face for the turns in the argument.

**Report:** time with a graphic on screen, target 40 to 60 per cent; longest stretch with no
visual change, target 12 seconds.

## What every format shares

Centre the speaker unless a deliberate layout says otherwise. Keep the bottom band clear for
the viewer's own captions. Cut to frames facing the lens. Use the project's theme. Prefer an
icon to a word where the meaning is obvious. Keep sound effects quiet and varied. Never
repeat one graphic shape so often that it becomes the answer to everything.
