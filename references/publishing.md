# Handing over

The edit is not the deliverable. What the person needs is a file they can upload and the
few decisions only they can make.

## What to produce

- **The masters.** `out/<name>-16x9.mp4`, and `out/<name>-9x16.mp4` if vertical was asked
  for. H.264, CRF 17, BT.709, faststart, AAC 256k.
- **Captions.** An SRT with the proper nouns corrected against the glossary. Upload it
  rather than relying on automatic captions, which will get the names wrong in exactly the
  places that matter.
- **Chapters**, if the video is over about eight minutes and has real sections.
- **Title and description options.** Three titles, not one, and say what each is doing
  differently — a question, a claim, a number. Descriptions carry the links.
- **Thumbnail candidates.** Frames from the master where the speaker's expression carries
  something, exported at full resolution. Not a title card.

Let them publish. Do not upload on someone's behalf.

## Filming once and publishing as parts

A common question: a long tutorial filmed in one sitting — one video, or several?

**Film once, publish as parts, then also publish the full-length version.**

Parts win on a video platform because discovery is per video. Ten parts give ten titles, ten
thumbnails and ten sets of search terms. Retention is measured as a percentage, so a
nine-minute part watched to the end reports far better than a ninety-minute video watched
for twelve minutes. And links have to land somewhere: a viewer who wants part 3 again needs
a URL that starts there, and timestamp links break the moment a re-edit moves them. A
playlist keeps the continuous experience for anyone who wants it.

Parts also win on a website with a page per part, because one part per page matches what the
reader is doing. One long video with changing text around it breaks as soon as anyone
scrubs, needs the player seeked per page, and puts a large file on every page.

Publishing the full version as well costs one extra upload and no extra editing, since it is
the parts concatenated.

**What this asks of the filming.** Each part must stand alone, which is a scripting job
rather than an editing one. While filming, say a clean opening line and a clean closing line
at each part boundary, even though it is one session. That costs nothing on the day and
stops the edit cutting at arbitrary points. The pipeline then runs: one recording, one edit
decision file, N part renders plus one full render.

## What to say at handover

Lead with the file path and the length. Then:

1. What changed since last time, in one list.
2. What the checks found, including anything that failed and what you did about it.
3. What could not be fixed in the edit, and how recording differently would fix it.
4. The decisions that are theirs: an alternative take you did not use, a claim you could not
   verify, a graphic you are unsure about.

Then the recording advice from the end of `craft-rules.md`. It is the cheapest way to
improve the next video and the only part of this they can act on themselves.
